# **Official Rulebook** beta 1.1

## **Welcome to Goon Squad Galaxy**

Goon Squad Galaxy is a head-to-head card game of violent attrition, tactical resource management, and ruthless efficiency. In this world, factions wage war using expendable bio-mechanical warriors known as Goons—organic, synthetic, or fused monstrosities who live only to serve their squad and protect their leader at all costs.

You are the commander of one such squad.

Your mission: deploy Goons from your hand, build your squad with calculated aggression, and eliminate your opponent’s forces—piece by expendable piece—until their most important piece, the **Squad Leader**, is left exposed and vulnerable. Once the Squad Leader falls, the game ends. Victory goes to the last leader standing.

But deploying isn’t free. It costs **wind**—the volatile mix of energy, trauma, and stress that builds inside every Goon until it breaks them. Push them too far, and they shatter. Play too cautiously, and your opponent will drown you in bodies.

This is a game about sacrifice. You will lose Goons. You’ll wind them up to pay for reinforcements. You’ll burn their corpses for parts. You’ll discard tools, cut off limbs, and twist the battlefield to your will.

Because in Goon Squad Galaxy, nothing is permanent.

**Except the cost of failure.**

---

## **What You’ll Need to Play**

To play Goon Squad Galaxy, each player will need:

* A **deck** of 52+ Goon cards, all from the same **faction**

* A clear **play area** with space for each player’s:

  * **Deck**

  * **Hand**

  * **Burn pile** (cards removed from the game)

  * **Deployed Goons** (face-up in play)

  * Access to the **shared Dead Pool** (discard pile for all destroyed Goons)

There are no tokens, dice, or counters in this game. Everything is managed by the cards themselves—specifically, through a unique mechanic called **Wind**.

---

## **Object of the Game**

Your goal is to **protect your Squad Leader** while building a team of Goons capable of overwhelming and dismantling your opponent’s defenses. Each player begins the game with their Squad Leader deployed face-up. These elite units are powerful and often have game-shifting abilities, but they are also your Achilles’ heel. If your Squad Leader is destroyed, you lose the game.

You will deploy Basic Goons to build numbers, Squad Goons to gain tactical advantages, and Titans to dominate the field. You’ll activate abilities, wind up your own Goons to pay deployment costs, and use the corpses of the fallen as raw material to summon reinforcements.

---

## **Factions**

There are two factions in Goon Squad Galaxy:

* **NARC Squad** – A criminal syndicate composed of drug-fueled mutants, chemically enhanced brawlers, and cybernetically reanimated freaks. Their playstyle is aggressive, chaotic, and dangerous.

* **PCU (Population Control Unit)** – A dystopian government agency deploying genetically optimized killing machines, clean cybernetics, and sterile tactical supremacy. Their playstyle is calculated, resource-efficient, and surgical.

When building a deck, all cards must come from a **single faction**. You cannot mix NARC and PCU cards in the same deck.

## **The Anatomy of a Goon**

Every card in the game is a **Goon**. From the lowest expendable foot soldier to the irreplaceable Squad Leader, all Goons share the same basic structure, both mechanically and physically. Understanding how to read a card is critical to mastering the game.

A Goon card contains the following components:

### **1\. Name**

The name of the Goon. Some effects will refer to Goons by name, so pay close attention.

### **2\. Portrait**

The artwork represents the unit—often grotesque, stylized, or intimidating. It has no mechanical purpose, but it helps bring the grim tone of the game to life.

### **3\. Rank Icon**

Located on the side panel, this icon tells you whether the Goon is a:

**Basic Goon (no rank icon):** Common units that can be deployed in multiples

**Squad Goon (SG):** More powerful units. Only one of each Squad Goon can be on the field at a time

**Squad Leader (SL):** Your commander. You start the game with one Squad Leader already in play

**Titan (T):** Massive, untargetable anomalies. Only one per deck

### **4\. Faction Icon**

Indicates whether the Goon belongs to the NARC Squad or the PCU. A deck can only include cards from a single faction.

NARC Squad

Population Control Unit

### **5\. Card Type(s)**

Located in the vertical side column, this shows whether the Goon is:

**Biological**

**Mechanical**

Both (a cyborg or hybrid)

Or neither (ghosts, anomalies, or other oddities)

These types are used for resource costs and card effects.

### **6\. Property Icons**

Passive traits like:

**Resist** – Reduces wind taken from abilities by 1

**No Unwind** – Prevents the Goon from healing wind at the start of your turn

### **7\. Deploy Cost**

The cost to bring this Goon into play. Some require **wind** from your current Goons. Others require **burning** Goons of certain types from the Dead Pool. Some require both.

Wind

Biological

Mechanical

### **8\. Abilities**

Each Goon may have one or more activated or passive abilities. These are the tools your squad will use to win the game—attacks, reactions, protection, buffs, and more. Each ability has its own cost, type, and effect.

### **9\. Flavor Text**

Optional text that adds thematic depth. It has no gameplay impact but helps tell the story behind your army.

## **Wind and Winding**

In Goon Squad Galaxy, every action has a cost. The core mechanic of this cost system is called **Wind**. *Pronounced like “grind”*

Wind represents the unstable cocktail of damage, exertion, mental decay, and bio-mechanical strain that builds up in every Goon. The more a Goon does—fighting, deploying others, using abilities—the more wind it takes on. And when a Goon takes on **too much**, it breaks.

Every Goon in the game has a maximum wind limit of **four**. Once it reaches four wind, it is immediately destroyed and sent to the **Dead Pool**.

### **Tracking Wind**

Rather than using dice or counters, wind is tracked **visually**, using the orientation of the card itself.

When a Goon accumulates wind, you physically **rotate the card 90 degrees counter-clockwise** for each point:

**0 wind**: Upright

**1 wind**: Turn left

**2 wind**: Upside-down

**3 wind**: Turn right

**4 wind**: Upright again — and then destroyed

This simple system makes it easy to track the health of your units at a glance. The rotation of the card is a visual metaphor for their deterioration—twisting, crumbling, and collapsing as the stress piles up.

Goons cannot exceed four wind. If a Goon is ever forced to gain a fourth wind, it immediately dies, even if that wind came from its own activation.

**Example:**

A Basic Goon with 3 wind activates an ability that costs 1 wind. You pay the cost, rotate it one final time… and it dies. The ability still resolves, but the Goon is immediately destroyed and sent to the Dead Pool afterward.

This opens up tactical decisions where Goons are deliberately sacrificed to secure a kill or force an ability through before they expire.

---

## **Unwinding**

At the start of every one of your turns, during the **Unwind Phase**, all of your Goons in play will **rotate 90 degrees clockwise**, reducing their wind by one.

This represents Goons resting, recalibrating, or stitching themselves back together—however your faction narratively handles recovery.

A Goon with 0 wind cannot unwind further. A Goon that is fresh (upright) stays that way unless it takes on wind again.

Some Goons have the **No Unwind** property. These units never rotate back and are locked in their current state until destroyed or removed. They’re often more powerful or aggressive, balanced by their inability to recover naturally.

**Example:**

You have a Squad Goon with 2 wind (upside-down). At the start of your turn, it unwinds back to 1 wind (turned left). If it has the No Unwind icon, however, it remains upside-down permanently until destroyed.

---

## **The Dead Pool**

The **Dead Pool** is a shared graveyard where all destroyed Goons go. It is not player-specific. The Dead Pool represents the universal scrap heap of the battlefield—twisted limbs, broken exo-suits, and bleeding parts, all waiting to be scavenged.

Whenever a Goon dies—by reaching four wind, being destroyed by an ability, or any other means—it is placed face-up into the Dead Pool, visible to both players.

The Dead Pool is not just symbolic. It’s a vital resource hub. Many cards and abilities interact with it directly, and most deployment costs rely on it.

---

## **Burning**

Some effects and deploy costs will require you to **burn** a card from the Dead Pool.

**Burning** is the act of permanently removing a Goon from the game. The card is taken from the Dead Pool and returned to its owner’s out-of-game pile (commonly called the Burn Pile).

There are two common resource types used for burning:

**Biological**: Any Goon with the Biological icon

**Mechanical**: Any Goon with the Mechanical icon

These types can be burned to satisfy costs.

**Important:** You can burn **any** valid Goon in the Dead Pool — not just your own. If your opponent’s dead Goon matches the resource type you need, you’re free to burn it.

**Example:**

You want to deploy a hybrid cyber-brute that requires 1 wind and 1 mechanical resource. You pay 1 wind by winding one of your active Goons, then burn a mechanical Goon from the Dead Pool—your own or your opponent’s.

---

## **Deployment**

Deploying a Goon means bringing it into play from your hand.

Goons are not free. Each Goon has a **cost**, shown in its vertical icon column, that must be paid to put it on the field. That cost may include any of the following:

1. **Wind Cost** – You must pay wind from Goons you already control. Wind is paid by rotating them 90° counter-clockwise for each wind spent.

    Only Goons that were already in play at the **start of your turn** can be used to pay wind costs.

2. **Biological / Mechanical Burn Costs** – You must burn one or more Goons from the Dead Pool, depending on the resource type required.

3. **Special Conditions** – Some Goons may have unique requirements, like needing a certain type of Goon already in play, or only being deployable once per game.

Once paid, the Goon enters play face-up, with 0 wind, in its upright orientation. However, newly deployed Goons cannot act immediately—they are **exhausted from arrival**, and must wait until your next turn to be used.

**Example:**

You have 3 Goons in play: two are upright, one is upside-down with 2 wind. You want to deploy a Squad Goon that costs 2 wind and 1 biological. You wind one upright Goon (1 wind), wind the upside-down Goon again (to 3 wind), and burn a biological Goon from the Dead Pool. You now deploy the Squad Goon fresh. However, it cannot activate abilities this turn—it must wait until your next Action Phase.

Deploying is one of the most common actions in the game—and one of the most dangerous. Winding your own Goons makes them weaker, edging them closer to death. Every deployment must be weighed carefully: are you reinforcing your team, or hastening your own collapse?

## **Deck Construction**

Before you can command a squad, you must build one.

Each player in Goon Squad Galaxy brings a custom-built deck of cards, representing the full extent of their faction’s tactical options. Decks must be built according to the following rules:

### **Faction Restriction**

A deck must contain cards from **only one faction**: either **NARC Squad** or the **PCU**. You cannot mix cards between factions. Thematically and mechanically, these forces are too different to cooperate.

Once you choose your faction, all Goons, Squad Goons, and Leaders in your deck must bear that faction’s icon.

### **Deck Size**

* Your deck must contain **at least 52 cards**.

* There is no upper limit to deck size, but consistency is key. Large decks dilute your odds.

### **Card Limits**

Certain card types have restrictions to preserve game balance:

* **Basic Goons (no rank icon)**: Up to **4 copies** of any card with the same name. You may control multiple copies of the same Basic Goon in play at once.

* **Squad Goons (SG)**: Also up to **4 copies per name** in your deck. However, you may only have **one of each named Squad Goon** in play at a time. If a second enters the field, the first is destroyed immediately.

* **Titans (T)**: These unique units are massive, powerful, and unorthodox. You may include **only one Titan** in your deck. Once destroyed, Titans **do not go to the Dead Pool** — they are removed from the game entirely.

* **Squad Leader (SL)**: You may have **one Squad Leader** in your deck. You do not draw or deploy them — your Squad Leader begins the game in play.

**Note:** Squad Leaders are unique, named characters. You may only include one per deck, and you do not need to pay to deploy them.

## **Game Setup**

When both players have built their decks and are ready to play, follow these steps to begin:

### **1\. Choose Factions and Decks**

Each player selects their pre-built deck from one of the two factions. Remember, no faction mixing is allowed.

### **2\. Place Your Squad Leader**

Each player places their Squad Leader **face-up in play** at the center of their side of the battlefield. No cost is paid — this is a free deployment. Squad Leaders begin the game upright with **zero wind**.

Your Squad Leader is your identity, your anchor, and your most valuable piece. Losing your Leader means losing the game.

### **3\. Shuffle and Draw**

Each player shuffles their deck thoroughly, then draws **six cards** as their opening hand.

If a player draws a hand they are unhappy with, they may mulligan — reshuffle and draw a new hand with **one fewer card**. This may be repeated, reducing the hand size by one each time, down to a minimum of **two cards**.

### **4\. Determine First Player**

Decide randomly who takes the first turn — by coin flip, die roll, or any mutually agreed method.

There is **no penalty** or card draw restriction for the first player.

### **5\. Prepare the Battlefield**

You are now ready to play. Each player should have:

* A **deck**

* A **hand** of six (or fewer) cards

* Their **Squad Leader** in play

* A space for deployed Goons (the **field**)

* A **Burn Pile** (out-of-game)  
* Shared access to the **Dead Pools**

## **Turn Structure**

A turn in Goon Squad Galaxy is broken into **four phases**, performed in this order:

### **1\. Draw Phase**

At the start of your turn, draw one card from your deck. There is **no hand size limit**. If you cannot draw because your deck is empty, you lose the game.

**Note:** If a card instructs you to draw during another phase, that draw happens in addition to your normal Draw Phase.

---

### **2\. Unwind Phase**

Each Goon you control that does **not** have the **No Unwind** icon rotates **90 degrees clockwise**, reducing its wind by one.

Goons that are upright already (0 wind) do not unwind further. Goons with No Unwind remain locked in place.

**Example:** You have a Squad Goon turned right (3 wind). It unwinds back to upside-down (2 wind). Another Goon with No Unwind remains upside-down at 2 wind.

Unwinding happens simultaneously to all eligible Goons under your control.

---

### **3\. Deploy Phase**

During this phase, you may deploy Goons from your hand by paying their full cost.

Each Goon’s cost may include:

* **Wind**: Paid by winding Goons that were on the field at the **start of your turn**

* **Biological / Mechanical burn costs**: Paid by burning appropriate Goons from the **Dead Pool**

* **Special conditions**: Some Goons may require specific traits or cards in play before they can be deployed

There is no limit to how many Goons you may deploy in a turn — so long as you can pay the full cost for each.

Newly deployed Goons enter the field **upright with 0 wind**, but **cannot activate abilities this turn**. They are considered exhausted from deployment and must survive until your next turn to be used.

**Important:** Goons deployed this turn **cannot** be used to pay wind costs or activate abilities until your next turn. Only Goons that were already in play at the start of this turn are eligible for action.

---

### **4\. Action Phase**

This is where the blood starts to flow.

During your Action Phase, you may activate **one ability per Goon** that was already in play at the start of your turn.

Each ability has its own cost shown in circles before ability text. That cost might include:

**Wind**: The Goon pays by taking on wind![][image1](rotating counter-clockwise)

**Burn Costs**: You burn Goons from the Dead Pool to satisfy biological![][image2] or mechanical![][image3] requirements

**Special conditions**: Some abilities require a specific target or situation to function

After paying the cost, resolve the ability fully. There are no “stack” or interruption mechanics in the core game — actions resolve immediately once activated.

Passive abilities (marked with a ♾️ symbol) do not count toward the one-ability-per-turn limit and remain continuously active while the Goon is in play.

**Example:**

Your Squad Goon “Bonewire Butcher” has an ability that costs 1 wind and 1 biological. You pay 1 wind by rotating the Goon, and burn a biological Goon from the Dead Pool. The ability resolves, dealing 2 wind to an enemy Goon.

Once all abilities have been used, or once you choose to end the phase, your turn is over. Your opponent begins their turn with a Draw Phase.

## **Abilities and Activation**

Abilities are at the heart of Goon Squad Galaxy. They define what your Goons can do — from dealing damage to summoning reinforcements, healing allies, or disrupting the opponent’s strategy.

Every Goon card has a text box that may contain one or more **abilities**. These abilities come in two forms: **activated** and **passive**.

### **Activated Abilities**

Activated abilities are used during your **Action Phase**, and may only be used by Goons who were on the field at the start of your turn.

Each activated ability includes: 

* A **cost** (usually wind and/or burn requirements  ![][image1]![][image2]![][image3] )  
* An **effect** (what the ability does) 

* A **target**, if applicable

To activate an ability:

1. Announce the Goon and the ability being used

2. Pay the cost (wind and/or burn)

3. Choose a legal target

4. Resolve the effect

A Goon may only use **one activated ability per turn**. You may not activate multiple abilities from the same Goon, even if you can afford the cost. Choosing when and how to use each Goon is a key part of your tactical play.

**Reminder:** Goons deployed this turn may **not** activate abilities. Only Goons present at the start of your turn are eligible.

---

### **Passive Abilities**

Passive abilities are always active, so long as the Goon is in play. These abilities are marked with a **♾️ icon** and do not require activation or payment.

They might grant your Goons protection, alter the rules of deployment, increase damage, or apply lingering effects to the battlefield.

Because passive abilities are continuous, they **do not count toward your one-ability-per-turn limit**, and they cannot be interrupted unless the Goon is destroyed.

**Example:** A Goon with the passive ability “♾️ All other Biological Goons you control gain Resist” will apply this effect the moment it enters play, and the effect remains until that Goon is removed from the field.

---

### **Ability Costs and Targeting**

Most abilities require payment of **wind**, **burned resources**, or both.

* **Wind** is paid by adding wind to the activating Goon (rotate counter-clockwise).

* **Burning** is paid by removing eligible Goons from the shared **Dead Pool**.

Abilities with a **target** must be directed at a legal target when activated. If no legal target exists, the ability cannot be used.

Some cards may use the word “choose,” “destroy,” “deal wind,” or “affect,” to describe their target interactions. These must all follow the targeting rules outlined below.

---

## **Targeting and the Protection Hierarchy**

One of the core defensive mechanics in Goon Squad Galaxy is the **Protection Hierarchy** — a simple but powerful targeting rule that governs how Goons may be attacked.

You **cannot** target a player’s **Squad Leader** with abilities or attacks **if they control any other Goons on the field**.

Until every other Goon — Basic, or Squad — has been destroyed or removed, the Squad Leader is **protected**.

This rule makes it impossible to win the game through a surgical strike or lucky hit. You must wear down the enemy’s defenses, pierce through the ranks, and leave their Leader exposed before going in for the kill.

**Example:** Your opponent controls three Basic Goons and their Squad Leader. You have an ability that targets an enemy Goon and destroys it. You **must** target one of the Basic Goons. Even if your Squad Leader could theoretically target theirs, the rules prevent you from doing so until they are alone.

This mechanic rewards strategic field control and proper sequencing of your attacks and deployments.

---

## **Titans**

Titans are a special type of Goon — colossal anomalies that defy the rules of warfare. They are unique, powerful, and dangerous, but they also come with limitations that prevent them from dominating the game unfairly.

Here’s how Titans work:

* You may include **only one Titan** in your deck

* You may have **only one Titan in play** at a time

* Titans **cannot be targeted** by abilities

* Titans **do not protect** your Squad Leader (they don’t count for Protection Hierarchy)

* When a Titan is destroyed, it is **removed from the game** (not placed in the Dead Pool)

Despite their size, Titans are expendable. They are forces of nature — called upon when all else fails, and discarded when their purpose is served.

Titans also cannot be used as fuel for future plays:

* You may **not wind a Titan** to pay wind costs

* You may **not burn a Titan** (they never enter the Dead Pool)

Titans are both a gift and a burden. Use them wisely.

---

## **End of the Game**

The game ends immediately when one of the following occurs:

1. A player’s **Squad Leader** is destroyed

2. A player must draw a card but **their deck is empty**

In either case, that player loses, and the opponent is declared the winner.

There are no tie conditions. There are no draws. There is only one survivor.

## **Glossary**

Here is a complete list of the terms used in Goon Squad Galaxy, with brief definitions for reference.

**Ability** – A Goon’s action or passive effect. Can be activated or passive (♾️).

**Activate** – Use a Goon’s ability during your Action Phase.

**Basic Goon** – A common Goon with no rank icon. Multiple can be in play.

**Biological (     )** – A Goon type. Can be burned for bio costs.

**Burn** – Remove a card from the game permanently by taking it from the Dead Pool.

**Burn Pile** – A personal out-of-game zone for burned cards.

**Dead Pool** – Shared discard pile for all destroyed Goons. Used as a resource pool.

**Deck** – Your personal draw pile. Minimum 52 cards.

**Deploy** – Bring a Goon from your hand into play.

**Deploy Cost** – Wind and/or burned resources required to play a Goon.

**Field** – The area where your deployed Goons are placed.

**Faction** – Either NARC Squad or PCU. A deck must contain cards from only one faction.

**Goon** – Any card in the game. From Basic to Squad Leader to Titan.

**Hand** – Your private set of drawn cards.

**Mechanical (     )** – A Goon type. Can be burned for mech costs.

**No Unwind** – A Goon cannot recover wind during the Unwind Phase.

**Passive Ability (♾️)** – Always in effect while the Goon is in play. Doesn’t count as an action.

**Resist** – Reduces wind damage from enemy abilities by 1\.

**Squad Goon (SG)** – Mid-tier units. Only one of each name may be in play.

**Squad Leader (SL)** – Unique commander unit. Begins in play. Losing it ends the game.

**Titan (T)** – Invulnerable, untargetable unit. Unique per deck. Removed from game when destroyed.

**Unwind** – During Unwind Phase, rotate a Goon clockwise to reduce wind.

**Wind** – Tracks a Goon’s damage/strain. At 4 wind, the Goon is destroyed.

**Winding** – Rotating a Goon counter-clockwise to pay for abilities or deployment costs.

---

## **Final Notes**

Goon Squad Galaxy is a brutal, tactical game where every decision matters — not just who to deploy or destroy, but when to let a Goon die, when to sacrifice strength for speed, and how much pressure your squad can handle before it cracks.

Whether you’re leading the anarchic, unstable NARC Squad or the cold, calculating PCU, one truth remains the same: you will lose Goons. That’s not failure — it’s strategy.

Build your squad. Burn your dead.

And do whatever it takes to keep your Leader alive.
